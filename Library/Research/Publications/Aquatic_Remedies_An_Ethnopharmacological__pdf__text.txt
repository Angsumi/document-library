  Study of Fish-Based Medicines in Assam's Tribal

       Ethnopharmacological relevance: This study illuminates the ethnopharmacological significance of fish-
       based remedies in Assam's tribal medicine, spotlighting the use of local fish for treating various ailments.
       By documenting these traditional practices,  it emphasizes the critical link between biodiversity and
        indigenous knowledge, urging conservation. The research offers a foundation for discovering new
        therapeutic agents, underscoring its importance in bridging traditional wisdom and modern pharmacology.


      Aim of the study: The aim of this study is to document and analyze the traditional use of fish in tribal
       medicine across Assam, identifying specific species utilized and their application in treating health
        conditions. Through gathering this indigenous knowledge, the research seeks to contribute to the
       ethnopharmacological field, highlighting the potential of these practices in modern medicine. Additionally,
            it aims to underline the importance of conserving both the aquatic biodiversity and the cultural heritage of
       Assam's tribal communities, advocating for a balanced approach to preserving traditional wisdom alongside
        ecological sustainability.


        Materials and methods: The study employed a qualitative research approach, focusing on Assam's tribal
       communities. Data were collected from the Bodo, Karbi, and Mishing tribes through semi-structured
        interviews, participant observations, and focus group discussions, targeting traditional healers, elders, and
        fishermen. Purposive sampling ensured a broad representation of medicinal practices. The areas chosen—
        Chariduar, Patgaon, and Pothailangso—were selected for their rich biodiversity and deep-rooted traditional
       medicine practices. Data analysis involved thematic categorization to identify recurring themes around fish
        species, their medicinal uses, and preparation techniques, aiming to consolidate indigenous knowledge on
        ichthyotherapy.


         Results: The study identified numerous fish species across the Bodo, Karbi, and Mishing tribes, utilized for
       a variety of health conditions like anemia, asthma, and weakness. Key species include Heteropneustes
          fossilis and Clarias magur, known for their wide medicinal applications. Preparation methods varied from
       consumption of cooked fish to application of raw parts. The findings underscore a rich tradition of
        ichthyotherapy, with specific fish playing crucial roles in tribal healthcare. The data reveal a deep reliance
      on indigenous ichthyofauna, highlighting the significance of these practices in the cultural and medicinal
        landscape of Assam's tribal communities.


        Conclusions: The study underscores the rich ethnopharmacological heritage of Assam's tribal communities,
        revealing a diverse array of fish-based remedies integral to their traditional medicine. It highlights the critical
         role of specific fish species in addressing a wide range of health issues, emphasizing the importance of
        preserving this indigenous knowledge alongside the aquatic biodiversity  it depends on. The findings
       advocate for further research into the bioactive compounds of these fish species, potentially contributing
        to new pharmacological discoveries. Additionally, the study calls for a balanced approach to conserving both
       the natural and cultural heritage, ensuring the sustainability of these traditional practices for future
        generations.

  Introduction
  Traditional medicine, encompassing the knowledge, skills, and practices indigenous to different cultures, has been
  utilized for health maintenance and the treatment of physical and mental conditions for centuries (Sophia et al., 2023).
 Among the myriad forms of traditional medicine, the use of aquatic organisms, particularly fish, in ethnomedicine, or
  ichthyotherapy, stands out as a unique and less explored domain (Grassberger & Sherman, 2013). This practice, rooted
  in the profound understanding of local biodiversity and ecological systems, underscores the symbiotic relationship
  between human communities and their natural environments (Sophia et al., 2023).


  Assam, a state in northeastern India, is renowned for its rich biodiversity and the vibrant cultural heritage of its tribal
  communities. These tribes have developed a deep connection with their surroundings, relying on the flora and fauna for
  sustenance, shelter, and medicine (Chakraborty, n.d.). Despite the growing body of research on ethnopharmacology in
  India, the medicinal use of fish by Assam's tribal villages remains under-documented, leaving a significant gap in the
  global compendium of traditional medicine (Teronpi et al., 2012).


  The present study aims to bridge this gap by documenting the Species of fish used as medicine, the ailments they are
  purported to treat, and the methods of preparation and administration of these aquatic remedies. Through this
  investigation, we seek to contribute to the broader understanding of ethnopharmacological practices, highlight the
  ethnomedical significance of biodiversity, and underscore the importance of preserving traditional knowledge in the face
  of rapid modernization and environmental change.


  This research not only enriches the ethnopharmacological literature but also opens avenues for the discovery of novel
  therapeutic agents, fostering a dialogue between traditional and modern medical practices. Moreover, by bringing to
  light the traditional uses of fish for medicinal purposes, this study advocates for the conservation of aquatic ecosystems,
  which are vital for the sustainability of these traditional practices.

  Literature Review
  The intersection of ethnopharmacology and traditional medicine offers a rich domain for exploring indigenous
  knowledge systems and their applications in healthcare. Ethnopharmacology, as a discipline, investigates the bioactive
  compounds present in natural resources traditionally used by ethnic groups, aiming to understand their medicinal
  properties and potential applications in modern medicine (Vontzalidou et al., n.d.). Within this framework, aquatic
  organisms, particularly  fish, have been identified as valuable but underexplored sources of therapeutic agents
  (Grassberger & Sherman, 2013).


  Globally, traditional medicine practices employing fish span various cultures and geographical regions, indicating a
  widespread recognition of their medicinal value. In Southeast Asia and parts of Africa, specific fish Species are utilized
  for their wound-healing properties, nutritional supplements during illness recovery, and treatments for a range of
  ailments including asthma, arthritis, and skin diseases (Garigliany et al., 2012). These practices underscore the diverse
  ethnomedical applications of fish, driven by local ecological knowledge and cultural traditions.


  In India, the use of fish in traditional medicine is documented across several states, reflecting the country's vast
  biodiversity and cultural diversity. Studies by (A. Das et al., 2021) in the northeastern region highlight the significance of
  riverine ecosystems to tribal communities, not only for sustenance but also for medicinal purposes. However, Assam,
  despite its rich biodiversity and significant tribal population, has seen limited documentation of its traditional medicinal
  practices, particularly those involving fish (Nasreen & Borah, 2023) (Teronpi et al., 2012)(BASUMATARY et al., 2023).


  The medicinal practices of Assam's tribal communities are deeply intertwined with their environment, with a reliance
  on natural resources for healthcare being a hallmark of their ethnomedicine(BORO et al., 2023). The state's diverse
  aquatic ecosystems provide a plethora of Species used in traditional remedies, yet the knowledge of these practices
  remains largely within the communities, at risk of being lost amidst rapid socio-environmental changes(Chetia et al.,
  n.d.)(Verma et al., 2014).

  This gap in documentation and understanding of Assam's traditional ichthyotherapy practices presents a critical area for
  research. By exploring the ethnomedicinal use of fish in Assam's tribal villages, this study aims to contribute to the
  conservation of indigenous knowledge, while offering insights into the potential for sustainable healthcare practices
  rooted in traditional wisdom. Moreover, it addresses the call by (M. Das et al., 2022) for interdisciplinary approaches to
  understanding the role of biodiversity in sustaining health and wellbeing in indigenous communities.


  In summary, while the global context provides a backdrop of the diverse uses of fish in traditional medicine, the specific
  practices of Assam's tribal communities remain underexplored. This study seeks to fill this gap, contributing to the
  broader ethnopharmacological discourse and supporting the conservation of both biological and cultural diversity.

 Methodology
  The present study adopted a qualitative research approach to investigate the utilization of fish in ethnomedicine among
  tribal communities in Assam. The research was carried out in three distinct locations: the Chariduar area of Sonitpur
  district (inhabited by the Bodo tribal people), Patgaon village in Sonitpur district (home to the Mishing tribe), and
  Pothailangso village in Biswanath district (where the Karbi tribal people reside). The selection of these areas was based
  on their abundant biodiversity, substantial tribal population, and long-standing reliance on traditional medicinal
  practices.


  Study Area and Population
  The study area focused on three tribal communities in Assam, namely the Bodo, Mishing, and Karbi peoples. Each
  community possesses a unique cultural heritage and traditional knowledge system, which includes specific methods of
  employing fish for medicinal purposes. The diverse geographical and ecological characteristics of the selected areas
  contribute to a comprehensive understanding of the ethnomedicinal practices prevalent in the region.


  Data Collection
  Primary data were gathered by employing a synthesis of ethnographic techniques, encompassing semi-structured
  interviews, participant observation, and focus group deliberations. The individuals involved in this study comprised
  traditional healers, elder members of the community endowed with profound insight into traditional medicine, and
  native fishermen who possess extensive familiarity with the therapeutic applications of fish.


  Semi-Structured Interviews: These interviews were conducted with traditional healers and knowledgeable elders in
  order to acquire comprehensive data regarding the various kinds of fish utilized, the ailments that are treated, as well as
  the methods of preparation and administration of fish-derived remedies.


  Participant Observation: This research method involved actively participating in community activities and closely
  observing the processes of collecting, preparing, and utilizing fish in traditional medicine practices.


  Focus Group Discussions: These discussions were facilitated among groups of community members with the purpose of
  validating and enhancing the information gathered through interviews and observations.


  Sample Selection
  To ensure a broad representation of knowledge on fish-based medicinal practices, participants were chosen using
  purposive sampling. The selection criteria included age (with a preference for elders possessing extensive knowledge of
  traditional practices), role in the community (such as traditional healers or fishermen), and willingness to share
  information.


  Data Analysis
  Data obtained from interviews, observations, and focus group discussions were meticulously transcribed and subjected
  to a comprehensive thematic analysis. This process entailed the meticulous categorization of the data into distinct groups
  that were centered around recurring themes pertaining to  fish Species, medicinal applications, techniques of
  preparation, and cultural relevance. The primary objective of this analysis was to discern patterns and establish
  correlations amongst the ethnomedicinal wisdom encompassing the three indigenous communities under study.

  The magur catfish, often referred to in the dataset as Clarius magur, which is likely a variant of Clarias magur, appears
  seven times. This species is well-known for its nutritional value and is commonly utilized in aquaculture. Its prominence
  in the dataset underscores its dual importance as a dietary staple and a medicinal resource, indicating its integral role in
  the tribal communities' health practices.


  The cuchia, Monopterus cuchia, an eel-like fish, is mentioned six times and is particularly notable for its use in treating
  anemia and conditions related to childbirth. This might be attributed to its high content of iron and other micronutrients,
  which are crucial for recovery and strength post-delivery.


  The dwarf snakehead, Channa gachua, appears four times, signifying its importance in treating ailments like swelling and
  dysentery. Its therapeutic use might be associated with its perceived potency and the active compounds found within its
  tissues.


  Lastly, the unspecified species of the genus Puntius (Puntius sp.) is mentioned four times and is used for a spectrum of
  conditions, including eye health and as a blood purifier. The recurrent mention of Puntius spp. reflects the tribes' broad-
  spectrum use of barbs for their perceived medicinal properties.


  The recurring reference to these species underscores their medicinal significance and the reliance on indigenous
  ichthyofauna for healthcare among the tribal communities of Assam. This highlights the intricate link between the cultural
  heritage of these tribes and the biodiversity they have historically accessed for their health and wellbeing.


 Conslusion


  The ethnopharmacological survey conducted in the tribal villages of Assam has culminated in a substantial inventory of
  fish species used as medicinal remedies. Key findings reveal a rich tradition of ichthyotherapy among the Bodo, Karbi, and
  Mishing tribes, with species such as Heteropneustes fossilis, Clarias magur, Monopterus cuchia, Channa gachua, and
  various species of the genus Puntius playing a significant role in the traditional healthcare practices of these communities.
  The study underscores the vast, yet largely undocumented, repository of traditional knowledge concerning the medicinal
  use of fish and highlights the critical role of ethnopharmacology in understanding and preserving these practices.


  Our findings reinforce the importance of ethnopharmacological studies in contributing to the body of traditional
  medicine, potentially providing insights for modern therapeutic approaches. The documentation of specific fish species

  Further research is recommended to isolate and characterize the bioactive compounds in these fish species, which may

  Preservation of this traditional knowledge is not without its challenges. The increasing exploitation of aquatic resources

  practices that allow for the continuation of traditional medicine while maintaining ecological balance.
